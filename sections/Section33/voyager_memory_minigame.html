Deep-space systems · Mission briefing

Bring Voyager 1’s signal back

Probe the memory blocks, identify what they do, and rebuild a clean, working layout. The puzzle is inspired by Voyager 1’s 2024 recovery: engineers worked around a fault in its flight data system by moving affected code in memory.

How to play

  1. Choose a mission tier, then probe blocks to inspect their source, telemetry, and reference notes. Use that evidence to identify each block.
  2. In Move mode, drag blocks to rearrange memory. Keep them inside the grid, with no overlaps and no occupied dead or reserved pages. Drop a block on the OFF rack to retire it.
  3. In Slice mode, cut a block along an inside edge. Click a glowing seam cell to stitch the fragments’ pointer back together; on harder tiers, you may need to enter the other fragment’s address.
  4. Watch the power budget and signal score. A clean layout boots Voyager; Medium and Hard also require a power-down and any in-progress stitch to be completed.

A note on the history

The 2024 recovery is the inspiration for this memory-repair puzzle, not a literal simulation of NASA’s commands. The game’s separate Truth check is about instrument power-downs: it awards its badge for retiring Camera, Plasma, and Cosmic Ray blocks, while recognizing that MAG and PWS remain valuable measurements.

Tip: Probe before retiring a block, and use the mission log and status indicators to see what the layout still needs.
